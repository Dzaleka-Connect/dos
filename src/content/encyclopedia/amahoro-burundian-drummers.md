## History

Amahoro Burundian Drummers was founded in 2006 by a group of young Burundian refugees at [Luwani Refugee Camp](/encyclopedia/luwani-refugee-camp) in Neno District, led by Stanislas Nshimirimana. When Luwani closed in 2007 and its residents were moved to Dzaleka, the group moved with them, and it has been based in the camp since.

The name — *amahoro*, Kirundi for "peace" — states the group's purpose: carrying a tradition of reconciliation into exile.

## Repertoire

The group performs the Burundian royal drum tradition, which UNESCO recognises as an Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. It uses heavy carved drums in the colours of the Burundian flag, played standing in ensemble with accompanying dance. According to the group, most of its current members were born in exile and learned the tradition in the camps rather than in Burundi.

## Performances

Coverage in Malawi's Nation newspaper and on Dzaleka.com records the group performing beyond the camp, including at national festivals, corporate events, and government functions, as well as at the [Tumaini Festival](/encyclopedia/tumaini-festival) in Dzaleka. It is one of several performance groups based in the camp, alongside [Salama Africa](/encyclopedia/salama-africa).
