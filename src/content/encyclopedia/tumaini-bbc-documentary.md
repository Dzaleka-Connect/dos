## Overview

***Tumaini*** is an international audio documentary produced by independent radio producer **Bairbre Flood** and broadcast on the **BBC World Service**. Recorded on-location inside Dzaleka Refugee Camp, the documentary offers an immersive auditory portrait of the annual **Tumaini Festival**—the world’s only major music and arts festival staged entirely within a refugee camp.

Combining live concert recordings, intimate tent interviews, spoken word poetry, and traditional percussion, the production captures how music functions as a powerful instrument of healing, intercultural bridge-building, and refugee dignity.

---

## Documentary Highlights

### 1. Voices of Dzaleka Artists
The documentary features in-depth interviews and acoustic performances from refugee artists representing the Democratic Republic of Congo, Rwanda, Burundi, and Somalia. Key featured acts include:
* **Trésor Nzengu Mpauni (Menes La Plume)**: Poet, musician, and founder of Tumaini Letu, discussing the vision of using art to demolish stereotypes surrounding refugees.
* **Amahoro Burundian Drummers**: Traditional Burundian sacred drumming ensemble performing high-energy rhythms inside camp venues.
* **Youth Spoken-Word Poets**: Young writers sharing poems on memory, displacement, hope, and community solidarity.

### 2. Intercultural Harmony and Host Integration
The BBC production highlights how the festival draws thousands of Malawian visitors from Lilongwe and surrounding Dowa villages into Dzaleka each year. Through shared musical celebration, the festival breaks down social barriers between refugees and host communities.

---

## Critical Reception and Awards

The documentary received wide praise across international broadcasting networks for portraying refugees not as passive victims of humanitarian crises, but as vibrant creators, organizers, and cultural leaders. It remains a major international media record of Dzaleka's artistic landscape.
