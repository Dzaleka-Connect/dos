## From Dzaleka to medical school

Mireille Twayigira left Rwanda with her family during the 1994 genocide. Jesuit Refugee Service records that she reached Dzaleka with her grandfather in 2000, at the age of eight, after years of displacement across several countries.

Twayigira attended a JRS-supported primary education programme in Dzaleka and later continued to secondary school. She graduated among Malawi's highest-performing female secondary students and received support from JRS and the Malawian and Chinese governments to study medicine in China.

She completed her medical degree in 2016 and returned to Malawi. Public profiles published by JRS and Georgetown University in 2018 and 2019 identified her as a medical doctor in Malawi.

## Education advocacy

Twayigira became a JRS Refugee Education Advocate, speaking publicly about the role education played in her life and arguing that displaced children should be treated as people with skills and agency rather than only as recipients of assistance.

Her connection to Dzaleka is central to that advocacy: the camp was where sustained access to school became possible after years of interrupted childhood education. Her public work has made her one of the best-documented examples of a former Dzaleka student entering medicine.
