## The directive

On 27 March 2023, the Malawi government issued a directive to enforce its [encampment policy](/encyclopedia/law-and-encampment-policy), which requires refugees and asylum seekers to live in Dzaleka. The directive ordered those living in urban and rural areas elsewhere in the country to return to the camp by 15 April 2023 or face relocation. [Human Rights Watch](https://www.hrw.org/news/2023/06/05/malawi-refugees-including-children-forcibly-relocated) noted that Dzaleka was already severely overcrowded and unable to meet the food, health, water, shelter, and sanitation needs of its existing population.

## Enforcement

From May 2023, authorities began detaining and relocating refugees and asylum seekers from across the country. According to Human Rights Watch, police and the military arrested men, women, and children in rural and urban districts, closed their businesses, held them temporarily in prisons, and then took them to Dzaleka. Some people arrested reported being beaten and having property destroyed or stolen.

## Documentation and aftermath

Human Rights Watch documented the relocations through 2023 and covered them in its [World Report 2024](https://www.hrw.org/world-report/2024/country-chapters/malawi) chapter on Malawi. In December 2023, it reported that a rights group had been expelled from the camp. The relocations added to the [overcrowding](/encyclopedia/housing-and-overcrowding) described in this encyclopedia's entries on housing and population, and are part of the encampment policy covered under law and policy.
