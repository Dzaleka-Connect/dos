## Overview

Dzaleka Refugee Camp is in Dowa District in Malawi's Central Region, approximately 41 kilometres from Lilongwe. It was established in 1994 to accommodate people displaced by conflicts in the Great Lakes region.

The camp was designed for between 10,000 and 12,000 people. UNHCR reported that it held more than 52,000 people at the end of 2024, making overcrowding a defining issue for housing, water, sanitation, education, health care, and public space.

## Community

Residents have come primarily from the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Burundi, and Rwanda, with smaller populations from Somalia, Ethiopia, and other countries. Dzaleka has developed schools, places of worship, markets, media, cultural organisations, sports groups, and refugee-led businesses alongside humanitarian services.

The settlement also serves and interacts with surrounding Malawian host communities. Facilities such as the health centre and cultural events such as Tumaini Festival have long brought camp residents and neighbouring communities into shared spaces.

## Administration and services

The Government of Malawi is responsible for refugee administration. UNHCR coordinates protection and assistance with government ministries, UN agencies, implementing partners, operational partners, and community organisations. The organisations involved and the scale of their programmes change over time, so current service details should be checked in the main service directory.

## Reading population figures

Population totals in reports differ by date and by whether they refer to Dzaleka itself or everyone registered in Malawi. Figures in this encyclopedia are always tied to a reporting date rather than presented as permanent totals.
