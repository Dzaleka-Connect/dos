## Why the study matters

The 2024 Malawi Demographic and Health Survey included Dzaleka Refugee Camp in a national household survey. A separate report prepared by Malawi's National Statistical Office and UNHCR used the data to compare Dzaleka with the rest of Malawi.

This is important because refugee statistics are often collected through parallel humanitarian systems. Inclusion in a national survey makes it possible to compare indicators using a common methodology and can help include displaced populations in national planning.

## Mixed findings

The Joint Data Center describes a mixed picture. Dzaleka recorded higher vaccination coverage, antenatal care, sanitation, birth registration, internet use, and male literacy than the national comparison.

At the same time, the analysis found severe pressure from overcrowding, low basic water access, limited employment, and higher unmet need for family planning. These findings should not be reduced to a single description of the camp as either better or worse than the national average; conditions vary by indicator.

## How to use the results

Survey estimates describe a population at a particular period and have margins of uncertainty. They do not replace current service records or individual medical advice. Their strongest use is in planning, comparing inequalities, identifying service gaps, and establishing a baseline for future surveys.
