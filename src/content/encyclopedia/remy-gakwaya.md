## Technology education

Remy Gakwaya came to Dzaleka from Burundi in 2008. He learned computer programming through courses supported by Jesuit Refugee Service at the Jesuit Worldwide Learning centre.

In 2016, Gakwaya founded TakeNoLab, a refugee-led technology learning group. UNHCR reported that its first students sometimes practised on printed keyboard diagrams because the group had too few computers and faced frequent power cuts.

## AppFactory

Gakwaya later taught at the Dzaleka AppFactory, which opened through a UNHCR and Microsoft partnership. The programme trained young people to build software around local needs. Projects reported by UNHCR included tools for new arrivals and information about distributions.

UNHCR's 2018 profile also recorded Gakwaya's work to involve more women and girls in technology training. These details describe the programme at the time of that report and should not be read as its current operating status.

## Community mapping

TakeNoLab was the local partner for MapMalawi's 2022 Dzaleka Mapping Project. It helped involve young people with knowledge of the camp in mapping buildings, water points, schools, health facilities, and other infrastructure.
