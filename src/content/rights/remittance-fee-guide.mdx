A **Remittance & Fee Guide** is critical for residents who rely on outside support. This guide helps you choose the most cost-effective channels and understand the latest 2026 fee structures.

## 1. International Remittance Channels (Receiving Money)

It is crucial to compare fees, as banks can be significantly more expensive than digital-first providers.

| Provider | Best For | Key Benefits |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **WorldRemit** | **Overall Value** | Often 41% cheaper than banks. Supports bank deposits, cash pickup, and mobile wallets. |
| **Remitly** | **Cash Pickup** | Frequently rated the cheapest provider for cash pick-up in Malawi (2026). |
| **Mukuru** | **Convenience** | "Mukuru Wallet" offers **free international cash-outs** at 250+ branches. |
| **Western Union** | **Availability** | Largest physical network, but often has higher fees than digital apps. |

## 2. Mobile Money Fees (Internal Transfers)

As of **January 1, 2026**, new taxes and levies apply.

### New Government Levy
A **0.05% levy** now applies to all e-money transfers greater than **K100,000**.

### Cross-Network Transfer Fees (Airtel ↔ TNM)
Sending money between networks (interoperability) costs more than same-network transfers.
*   **K30,000 – K49,999**: ~K1,765 fee
*   **K100,000 – K199,999**: ~K4,165 fee

> **Limit:** Most personal wallets have a holding limit of **K1,000,000**.

## 3. Banking & Foreign Currency

### Refugee ID vs. KYC
**Good News:** UNHCR-issued IDs are increasingly recognized by banks for Know-Your-Customer (KYC) requirements to open accounts and register SIM cards.

### Diaspora Accounts
The Reserve Bank of Malawi has introduced "Diaspora Foreign Currency Accounts." While currently for citizens, this signals a shift towards easier foreign currency handling.

> [!WARNING]
> **Cash Transfer Risk**: WFP/Aid cash transfers restricted to licensed shops may carry a **16%–30% premium** compared to cash prices. Always compare prices.
