## Navigating Education Opportunities in Dzaleka Refugee Camp

Education is a cornerstone of hope and opportunity for residents of Dzaleka Refugee Camp. Despite challenges such as overcrowding and limited resources, a range of educational pathways exists for children, youth, and adults. Here's how you can find, enroll in, and support educational journeys in Dzaleka:


### Available Schools and Programs

**Preschool/Early Childhood Education**
- Community-based preschools provide young children with a safe, nurturing start, focusing on social, emotional, and cognitive development through play-based learning.
- Programs help children prepare for primary school and establish routines that foster stability.

**Primary Education**
- Primary schools in Dzaleka follow the Malawian national curriculum, teaching literacy, numeracy, and life skills in both Chichewa and English.
- Despite high demand and overcrowded classrooms, efforts are made to include as many children as possible, with special programs for out-of-school children.

**Secondary Education**
- Secondary schools offer formal education aligned with national standards, preparing students for national exams and further study.
- Space is limited, so not all primary graduates can continue. Alternative and remedial programs exist for those who missed earlier education.

**Post-Secondary & Vocational Training**
- Opportunities include certificate and degree programs through partnerships with universities and online platforms, as well as vocational training in trades like tailoring and entrepreneurship.
- Vocational programs are especially important for youth and women, equipping them with practical, marketable skills.

**Adult Education**
- Adult literacy and continuing education programs are available, helping adults gain essential skills for daily life and employment.



### Scholarships and Support

- Some organizations and initiatives offer scholarships and academic support for secondary and post-secondary studies, including financial aid and mentorship.
- Special projects like the Naweza Project focus on empowering girls and improving access to education through sanitary packs, WASH facilities, and community engagement.

### How to Enroll or Support a Child's Learning Journey

1. **Visit the Nearest School or Learning Center:**  
   Ask about enrollment periods, required documents, and available space.

2. **Contact Community Education Partners:**  
   Organizations such as Jesuit Refugee Service (JRS), UNHCR, and local NGOs can provide information on programs and scholarships.

3. **Apply Early:**  
   Due to high demand, enroll as soon as possible and follow up regularly.

4. **Support Attendance:**  
   Encourage regular attendance, help with homework, and participate in school meetings or parent groups.

5. **Explore Alternative and Supplementary Programs:**  
   For those unable to access formal schooling, look for community-based education, tablet-based learning, or vocational programs.

### Overcoming Barriers

- Dzaleka's education system faces significant challenges: overcrowded classrooms, limited infrastructure, and shortages of teachers and materials.
- Community and international partners are working to expand access, improve quality, and address gender disparities.
- Your involvement—as a student, parent, or supporter—makes a difference in overcoming these barriers and building a brighter future for all.

Your involvement—whether as a student, parent, or supporter—contributes to building a brighter future for Dzaleka's residents. For more information about educational opportunities, visit the education resources section on Dzaleka Online Services or contact local education partners in the camp.
